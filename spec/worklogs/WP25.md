# WP25 — Magic type, Magic rating, tradition selection, and the Talent free-skill grant

Status: complete
Ref: [Implementation Plan.md](../Implementation%20Plan.md) — Phase I, WP25.

## Goal

Let the Talent letter's real *option* be chosen (Mundane/Magician/Adept — this phase's offered
set, per Phase I's intro) and derive `Character`'s Magic rating, Power Points, and (Magician only)
tradition and the Talent-granted free skill(s). Engine + data layer only — WP24 already made
Talent a real 5th priority letter in the web layer with zero new capability; WP29 is where this
WP's engine work reaches the browser.

## Log

### 2026-07-19 — Verifying the free-skill grant against real data caught two errors in the
Implementation Plan's own research

Before writing `create_magic_selection`, I checked every A-D Talent letter's `<skillqty>`/
`<skillval>`/`<skilltype>` directly against `priorities.xml` rather than trusting the Plan's
one-line summary ("Adept letter B grants 1 at rating 4, letters C/D grant none"):

```
A Magician: skill_qty=2, skill_val=5, skill_type=magic
B Adept:    skill_qty=1, skill_val=4, skill_type=xpath
B Magician: skill_qty=2, skill_val=4, skill_type=magic
C Adept:    skill_qty=1, skill_val=2, skill_type=xpath   <- the Plan says this doesn't exist
C Magician: skill_qty=None (no grant at all)
D Adept:    skill_qty=None (no grant at all)             <- this one the Plan gets right
```

**Adept letter C does grant a free skill** — same `xpath` shape as letter B, just at rating 2
instead of 4. This isn't a hand-wavable edge case: Wesson (Adept, Talent letter C) is one of this
phase's only two real Priority-built fixtures, and his own `.chum5` directly contradicts the Plan's
adjacent claim ("Wesson's Talent letter, C, grants no free skill at all") —
`<priorityskills><priorityskill>Pistols</priorityskill></priorityskills>` is right there in the raw
file. `chummer5.io.legacy_chum5` had already parsed this (`PrioritySelections.talent_bonus_skills`,
added in WP4 for round-trip completeness, never consumed until now) — I didn't need to add any new
parsing to discover it, just actually look at what it returned for Wesson. Pistols is Combat Active
(AGI-linked, not RES/DEP, no skill-group exclusion since it isn't Magical Active), so it cleanly
satisfies the xpath predicate — this is a real, second confirmation of the free-skill mechanism
(alongside Miko's `Counterspelling`/`Spellcasting` for the `magic` skilltype), not just a
plan-correction footnote. Both `tests/unit/test_magic.py` and `tests/integration
/test_magic_fixtures.py` assert the corrected letter-C-grants-a-skill behavior directly, and
`spec/rulebooks/learnings.md`'s new WP25 entry records the correction so it isn't silently
re-introduced by a future WP reading only the Plan text.

This also means Miko's and Wesson's `<priorityskills>` *names* are usable as real ground truth for
"which skills got granted" (not just "the mechanism is right by inspection of the C#", which is
what the Plan's acceptance criteria assumed was the best available check) — even though, per the
Plan's own correct observation, the resulting `<base>` *totals* still commingle the grant with
regular Skills-priority spend and can't be used to check the rating math end-to-end. Both
integration tests assert the granted names/ids, not the totals.

## Design decisions

**Correction (2026-07-20, recorded in `spec/worklogs/WP29.md`):** this WP's own core finding —
"the Magic rating is not a point-buy" — was wrong. Special Attribute Points genuinely can be split
between Edge and Magic/Resonance (core rulebook p.65, confirmed against the legacy engine too); it's
only correct that MAG isn't funded from the Attributes-priority budget the primary 8 attributes use.
Neither of this phase's 2 real fixtures caught the error because both happened to land on a letter/
codename combo already at the metatype's Magic maximum, leaving no headroom to spend into. See
`spec/worklogs/WP29.md`'s "Correction" section and `spec/rulebooks/learnings.md`'s "Post-WP29
correction" entry for the full fix. The below is left as originally written for the historical
record of what was decided and why at the time.

- **`MagicSelection` is a validation-time return value, not a field `Character` stores wholesale.**
  The Implementation Plan explicitly names two new `Character` fields (`magic_type`, `tradition`)
  rather than one wrapping selection object (contrast WP26/WP27's phrasing, "`Character` gains
  `spell_selection: SpellSelection | None`" / "`power_selection: PowerSelection | None`" — a
  deliberate difference, not an oversight, since I re-read the Plan text specifically to check
  this). I took that literally and added two more flat fields beyond the named pair —
  `magic_rating: int = 0` and `magic_granted_skill_ids: tuple[str, ...] = ()` — because `magic_type`
  alone has nowhere to keep the actual MAG number or which skills were granted. Flat fields (not a
  wrapping `magic_selection: MagicSelection | None`) because, unlike `skill_allocation`/
  `quality_selection`/etc. (each read back out as one bundle, once per render), `magic_type`/
  `magic_rating` are each read individually and often — `attribute_value` dispatches on
  `magic_type` for *every* MAG-linked skill's dice pool, not once per page load — so an `if
  character.magic_selection` unwrap at every call site would be pure friction. `create_magic_
  selection` is still the one validated construction path; a caller unpacks its `MagicSelection`
  onto `Character`'s flat fields via `dataclasses.replace`, mirroring `MetatypeChoice`'s existing
  relationship to `Character.metatype`/`heritage_letter`/etc.
- **MAG resolution is a special case inside `attribute_value`, not a new method.** The Plan asked
  for an explicit choice here. A new method (e.g. `magic_value()`) would require every caller to
  know to call it instead of `attribute_value("MAG")` — but `chummer5.engine.skills.dice_pool`
  already calls `character.attribute_value(skill.attribute)` generically for *every* skill,
  including Magical Active ones, for any character, magic or not. Special-casing inside
  `attribute_value` means that caller needs zero changes and automatically gets the right answer.
  A pleasant side effect, not the motivation but worth recording: this is a real correctness fix,
  not just an addition — before this WP, `attribute_value("MAG")` for a Mundane character (the only
  kind that existed) fell through to the generic formula and returned the *metatype's raw MAG
  eligibility minimum* (1, for every MVP metatype — `metatypes.xml` doesn't encode "Talent forced
  to E" at the metatype level), not the correct 0. Nothing in the existing suite exercised this
  (no fixture/test ever computed a dice pool for a Magical Active skill pre-WP25, since Talent was
  hardcoded to Mundane and no such skill was ever offered/spent), so this was a latent gap rather
  than a regression — closed as a side effect of doing MAG resolution properly rather than as its
  own fix.
- **Power Points is a computed `@property`, not a stored field.** Nothing about it varies
  independently of `magic_type`/`magic_rating` (mirrors `BASE_ESSENCE`'s own "compute, don't
  duplicate" precedent, and WP23's explicit choice not to persist `metatype_qualities` for the same
  reason) — storing it separately would just be redundant, staleness-prone state with no upside.
- **The Talent free-skill grant is applied as a plain `Improvement`, not a new field on
  `SkillAllocation`.** The Plan asked this to be decided explicitly: "a granted rating is not
  bought with Skills-priority points, so it must not double-count against that budget, but it
  should still show up as the skill's real total rating." `chummer5.engine.skills.skill_rating`
  already computes `points (from SkillAllocation) + Σ improvements (from ImprovementIndex)` — the
  exact shape needed. Routing the grant through `ImprovementType.SKILL` (the same mechanism
  `chummer5.engine.qualities` already established for flat skill bonuses, just a different
  `ImprovementSource`) means `SkillAllocation`'s own budget-validation code needs zero changes and
  has no way to accidentally see the grant — a new `SkillAllocation` field would have required
  either exempting it from the budget check (an easy place to introduce a bug later) or threading
  a second parallel accounting path through `create_skill_allocation`. New `ImprovementSource
  .TALENT` member added so the grant's source is distinguishable from `QUALITY` (matters if a
  future WP needs to selectively retract just the Talent grant via `remove_source`).
- **One shared `source_id` (`"magic_skill_grant"`) covers every skill a single grant touches**,
  not one id per granted skill. There's only ever one Talent selection (and therefore one grant)
  per character, so a single id is sufficient for a future "Talent changed, retract the old grant"
  `remove_source` call to clear the whole thing in one shot — matching the granularity of the thing
  being retracted (one grant) rather than the granularity of what it targets (N skills).
- **`create_magic_selection` handles all 3 offered codenames (Mundane/Magician/Adept) in one
  function, not a Mundane bypass plus a separate Magician/Adept path.** This mirrors every other
  `create_*` function in the engine (`create_quality_selection`, `create_skill_allocation`, ...): a
  single validated entry point that raises `ValueError` for anything invalid, rather than two
  different call shapes a caller has to choose between. Concretely this means Mundane must still be
  validated against the *offered* option list for whatever letter was passed in (today that's only
  ever letter E, since no other A-D letter's Talent list has a "Mundane" entry — confirmed via
  WP24's own research, re-confirmed here), not silently accepted at any letter.
- **`chummer5.data.traditions` filters to `source == "SR5"` and excludes `"Custom"` at load time**,
  mirroring `chummer5.data.qualities.load_qualities()`'s "filter at the loader, not downstream"
  precedent rather than `chummer5.data.metatypes`' "load everything, filter in the engine layer"
  precedent. Traditions has no consumer that would ever want the unfiltered (or Custom-inclusive)
  list — unlike metatypes, which `chummer5.io.legacy_chum5`/`legacy_import` need unfiltered to
  cross-reference a *real* imported character's metatype name (including non-SR5 ones, to produce
  a specific rejection reason) — so filtering at the loader is simpler with no real downside here.

## Implementation

- `src/chummer5/data/traditions.py` (new): `Tradition` (id, name, `drain_attributes: tuple[str,
  str]`, source, page); `load_traditions()` parses `traditions.xml`, filtered to `source == "SR5"`
  and excluding `"Custom"` (2 of 3 entries: Hermetic, Shamanic). `_parse_drain_attributes` extracts
  the two brace-wrapped attribute codes out of `<drain>`'s formula string (`"{WIL} + {LOG}"` ->
  `("WIL", "LOG")`) via a small regex — confirmed this shape is uniform across every SR5 tradition
  entry before writing the parser, not assumed.
- `src/chummer5/engine/improvements.py`: `ImprovementSource` gains `TALENT`.
- `src/chummer5/engine/character.py`:
  - `Character` gains `magic_type: str | None = None`, `magic_rating: int = 0`,
    `tradition: Tradition | None = None` (imported under `TYPE_CHECKING`, same circular-import
    reasoning as the other selection types), `magic_granted_skill_ids: tuple[str, ...] = ()`.
  - `attribute_value` special-cases `code == "MAG"` to return `self.magic_rating` directly, before
    falling through to the existing metatype-range formula for every other attribute (see Design
    decisions for why this is a dispatch point, not a new method).
  - New `power_points` property: `magic_rating if magic_type == "Adept" else 0`.
  - Class docstring gains a WP25 paragraph explaining all of the above, including why this is 4
    flat fields rather than one wrapping selection.
- `src/chummer5/engine/magic.py` (new):
  - `OFFERED_TALENT_CODENAMES = ("Mundane", "Magician", "Adept")` — this phase's scope cut, applied
    as a name filter.
  - `offered_talent_options(letter, priorities)` — one letter's real option list, restricted to the
    above.
  - `magic_skill_grant_eligible_skills(talent_option, skills)` — dispatches on
    `talent_option.skill_type` (`"magic"` -> category filter; `"xpath"` -> the one traced predicate;
    `None` -> `()`; anything else -> `ValueError`, a guard against a future Talent option this
    phase's traced predicates don't cover).
  - `MagicSelection` (frozen dataclass): `magic_type`, `magic_rating`, `tradition`,
    `granted_skill_ids`, `granted_skill_rating`.
  - `create_magic_selection(talent_letter, talent_codename, tradition_id, granted_skill_ids,
    priorities, traditions, skills)` — the single validated construction path (see Design
    decisions for the full validation list per codename).
  - `apply_magic_skill_grant_improvements(character, selection, skills)` — adds one
    `Improvement(SKILL, skill.name, value=granted_skill_rating, source_type=TALENT,
    source_id="magic_skill_grant")` per granted skill.
- `tests/fixtures/mundane_chum5.py`: new `MAGIC_PRIORITY_BUILD_FIXTURES = ("Miko.chum5",
  "Wesson.chum5")`, documented alongside the existing 11-fixture registry.
- `tests/fixtures/README.md`: new "Phase I" section documenting the 2 new fixtures and why they're
  separate from `MUNDANE_FIXTURES`.

## Tests

- `tests/unit/test_traditions.py` (new): offered-set (`{Hermetic, Shamanic}`), each tradition's
  `drain_attributes`, default-path sanity.
- `tests/unit/test_magic.py` (new, 21 tests): Mundane (no magic, rejects a tradition, rejects a
  skill grant); Magician (letter A's full resolution incl. tradition + 2-skill grant, tradition
  required, unknown tradition id rejected, letter C's no-grant case, ineligible/wrong-count/
  duplicate skill-grant rejections); Adept (letter C's corrected grant — see Log — cannot choose a
  tradition, xpath-predicate rejections for a grouped-Magical-Active skill and a RES-linked skill,
  letter D's no-grant case, "not offered at letter A"); scope-cut guards (Mystic Adept/Technomancer
  never offered at any letter); `magic_skill_grant_eligible_skills` unit tests for both skill_types
  plus the unrecognized-type `ValueError`; an end-to-end check that
  `apply_magic_skill_grant_improvements` makes a granted skill's `dice_pool` reflect the grant with
  an *empty* `SkillAllocation` (proving the budget really isn't touched).
- `tests/unit/test_character.py`: 2 new tests — a Mundane character's `attribute_value("MAG")` is 0
  despite the metatype's raw range allowing 1 (the latent-gap fix, see Design decisions), and
  `power_points` is 0; a `dataclasses.replace`d Magician/Adept's `attribute_value("MAG")` returns
  the stored rating directly (not metatype-min-plus-points), with `power_points` correctly 0 for
  Magician and equal to `magic_rating` for Adept.
- `tests/integration/test_magic_fixtures.py` (new): Miko (Magician) — resolves letter A, MAG
  rating 6 matches her own `<attribute name="MAG"><totalvalue>`, granted-skill ids match her real
  `<priorityskills>` names (`{Counterspelling, Spellcasting}`) at rating 5. Wesson (Adept) —
  resolves letter C, MAG rating 4 matches his `<totalvalue>`, granted-skill id matches his real
  `Pistols` grant at rating 2, no tradition. Both assert the ids/rating the mechanism itself
  produces, not the fixtures' commingled `<base>` totals (see Log for why those can't be used).

## Outcome

WP25 complete. `chummer5.engine.magic.create_magic_selection` resolves a validated Magic-type +
Magic-rating + tradition + Talent-free-skill-grant selection for Mundane/Magician/Adept, composed
onto `Character` via 4 new flat fields and a special-cased `attribute_value("MAG")` rather than
forcing MAG into the point-buy `ALLOCATABLE_ATTRIBUTES` shape EDG uses. The free-skill grant reuses
the existing `ImprovementType.SKILL` mechanism, so it reaches a skill's real dice pool without ever
touching `SkillAllocation`'s budget accounting. Both of the corpus's real magic-capable Priority
fixtures (Miko, Wesson) resolve their exact Magic rating and free-skill-grant names correctly —
Wesson's case corrects a genuine error in the Implementation Plan's own prior research (Adept letter
C does grant a free skill; the Plan claimed it doesn't), caught by checking `priorities.xml` and his
own `.chum5` directly rather than trusting the Plan's summary. `pytest`/`ruff`/`mypy` are all green
(355 tests, 30 more than before this WP). WP26 (spell selection) and WP27 (Adept powers) build on
top of this directly; WP29 is where Talent/tradition selection reaches the browser.
