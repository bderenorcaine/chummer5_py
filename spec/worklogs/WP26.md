# WP26 — Spell selection (Magician)

Status: complete
Ref: [Implementation Plan.md](../Implementation%20Plan.md) — Phase I, WP26.

## Goal

Let a Magician pick spells up to their Talent option's `spells` budget (Miko: 10, at letter A).
Engine + data layer only, mirroring WP25's own scope — WP29 is where this reaches the browser.

## Log

### 2026-07-19 — Confirming `spells.xml`'s shape before writing the loader

Checked `legacy/Chummer/data/spells.xml` directly: 363 raw `<spell>` entries, 93 with
`source == "SR5"` — matches the Implementation Plan's own count exactly, so no correction needed
here (unlike WP25's free-skill-grant research). Every SR5 entry shares one of two field sets:
`{category, damage, descriptor, duration, dv, id, name, page, range, source, type}`, plus a
`<bonus>` child on 7 of the 93 (the `Detect [...]`/`Increase [Attribute]`/`Decrease [Attribute]`
family — a player-facing in-play target/parameter choice, e.g. "which attribute", not a chargen-time
Improvement grant). Confirmed by reading a few of those 7 directly (`<bonus><selectattribute>...`,
`<bonus><selecttext/>`) — none of them describe a permanent stat bonus the way a quality's `<bonus>`
does, so `chummer5.data.spells` doesn't parse `<bonus>` at all, matching the Implementation Plan's
own framing ("a spell is a roster entry, not a computed value. No casting-test/drain-resolution
modeling needed at chargen").

Cross-checked `priorities.xml`'s Magician `<talent>` entries for every offered letter (A/B/C — this
phase excludes Aspected Magician/Apprentice/Explorer/Enchanter/Aware, see the Phase I intro):
`spells` is 10/7/5 respectively, always present for Magician and always absent (`None`) for every
Adept entry at any letter — confirming a spell budget is a pure Magician concept, with nothing for
`create_spell_selection` to special-case per `magic_type` itself (see Design decisions below for why
the function takes a plain `budget: int` instead).

### 2026-07-19 — No new rulebook research needed this WP

WP25's own rulebook pass (p.68, "Step Three: Choose Magic or Resonance") already covers the exact
sentence this WP's mechanism rests on — "Magicians... automatically know a number of... spells...
as listed in the appropriate box... they do not cost any skill points or Karma up front" (see
`spec/rulebooks/learnings.md`'s WP25 entry) — so this WP doesn't add a new citation there; it's
already on record and directly answers the one question that mattered ("is the spell grant free and
unconditional, with a fixed count"). Re-confirmed the citation still applies before relying on it
rather than re-deriving it from scratch.

### 2026-07-19 — Miko's own `<spells>` block only partially exercises the SR5-scoped catalog, the
same shape as her tradition

Read Miko's `.chum5` `<spells>` block directly (bare `xml.etree.ElementTree`, not through
`chummer5.io.legacy_chum5` — that module has never parsed spells; adding a real parser is WP28's
job per the Implementation Plan's Phase I intro, not this one's). All 10 entries are present as
expected (matching her Talent option's `spells=10` budget), but only 6 carry `source == "SR5"`
(Manabolt, Stunbolt, Clout, Improved Invisibility, Cure Disease, Heal); the other 4 (Calm Animal,
Animal Sense, Mindnet Extended, Fashion) are `source == "SG"` — the same non-core sourcebook as her
Shinto tradition (see WP25's worklog/module docstring for that precedent). This means Miko's
fixture, while real ground truth for "the free-skill/spell grant mechanism is unconditional and
sized correctly," cannot exercise a full 10-of-10 catalog resolution the way, say, WP21's gear
catalog got exercised against a fixture — the integration test asserts the 6/4 split explicitly
(via the raw XML's own `<source>` tags) rather than only checking the 6 that happen to work, so the
gap is visible in the test itself, not just in prose.

## Design decisions

- **`create_spell_selection` takes a plain `budget: int`, not a `TalentOption` or `magic_type`
  parameter.** Mirrors `create_resources_selection`'s existing `lifestyle_cost: int` shape (a
  pre-resolved number handed in by the caller) rather than `create_magic_selection`'s own
  `talent_letter`/`talent_codename`-plus-lookup shape. The reason for the difference: `create_magic_
  selection` has to *resolve* which Talent option applies (letter + codename together determine it,
  and validating that resolution — is this codename really offered at this letter — is exactly its
  job). Spell selection has nothing analogous to resolve; by the time a caller has a `MagicSelection`
  in hand, `option.spells` (Adept/Mundane: `None`, treated as `0`) is already known, so threading the
  whole `TalentOption` (or re-deriving it a second time) through `create_spell_selection` would just
  be indirection with no validation benefit. `budget=0` already produces the correct rejection for
  any non-empty pick, so there's no missing case a `magic_type` parameter would have caught.
- **No `apply_spell_selection_improvements` function, unlike every other selection type this
  project has built (qualities, the Talent skill grant, ...).** Not an oversight — a spell has no
  chargen-time computed effect at all (confirmed while reading the 7 `<bonus>`-carrying spells, see
  Log): it never targets `ImprovementIndex`, never changes a dice pool, an attribute, or a limit.
  `SpellSelection` exists purely as a validated roster, the same shape `Contact` already has (no
  catalog, no engine-computed value, just a player-authored/catalog-picked list recorded on the
  character).
- **`SpellSelection` is a single wrapping `Character.spell_selection` field, not flattened the way
  WP25's `magic_type`/`magic_rating`/etc. were.** WP25's own docstring explains why *those* fields
  were flattened: they're each read individually and often (`attribute_value` dispatches on
  `magic_type` for every MAG-linked skill). Nothing anywhere reads `spell_selection` piecemeal — it's
  read back out as one bundle for display, the same access pattern `quality_selection`/
  `contact_selection` already have — so this WP follows *their* precedent instead, not WP25's.
- **`SpellSelection.budget` is stored on the selection itself**, even though it's also derivable from
  `TalentOption.spells` at any later point. Consistent with `ResourcesSelection` (which stores
  `nuyen_budget` alongside `gear`) rather than with `power_points` (a computed `@property`, no
  stored duplicate) — the difference is that `ResourcesSelection`/`SpellSelection` are themselves
  already "the record of one validated construction," so storing the budget they were checked
  against is documentation of *that* construction's inputs, not new derivable state layered on top
  of `Character` the way a second `power_points`-style property would be.

## Implementation

- `src/chummer5/data/spells.py` (new): `Spell` (id, name, category, damage, duration, dv, range,
  type, descriptor, source, page — every field `spells.xml` carries for an SR5 entry, all kept
  descriptive/uninterpreted, per the "no in-play resolution system" framing above); `load_spells()`
  parses `spells.xml`, restricted to `source == "SR5"` (93 of 363). No `<bonus>` parsing at all — see
  Log for why.
- `src/chummer5/engine/magic.py`:
  - `SpellSelection` (frozen dataclass): `spells: tuple[Spell, ...]`, `budget: int`.
  - `create_spell_selection(spell_ids, catalog, budget)` — validates every id resolves against
    `catalog`, no duplicates, total picked <= `budget`. See Design decisions for why `budget` is a
    plain int rather than a `TalentOption`/`magic_type` pair.
- `src/chummer5/engine/character.py`: `Character` gains `spell_selection: SpellSelection | None =
  None` (imported under `TYPE_CHECKING` from `chummer5.engine.magic`, same circular-import pattern
  every other selection type already uses); class docstring gains a WP26 paragraph.

## Tests

- `tests/unit/test_spells.py` (new): loader count (93, all `source == "SR5"`); category distribution
  hand-checked against the raw XML (Illusion 19/Combat 18/Detection 18/Manipulation 18/Health
  11/Rituals 9); Fireball's full field set and Manabolt/Heal's key fields hand-checked; no duplicate
  ids.
- `tests/unit/test_magic.py`: 7 new tests for `create_spell_selection` — within-budget selection
  matches the expected `SpellSelection` exactly; partial spend allowed (mirrors every other `create_
  *` function's "don't require the full budget spent" rule); unknown id rejected; duplicate rejected;
  over-budget rejected with the exact count in the message; `budget=0` rejects any non-empty pick
  (the Adept/Mundane case, checked without needing a `magic_type` parameter); `budget=0` with an
  empty pick succeeds.
- `tests/integration/test_magic_fixtures.py`: new
  `test_miko_spell_selection_resolves_her_sr5_spells_and_flags_the_rest` — reads Miko's `<spells>`
  block via a bare `ElementTree` parse (not through `chummer5.io.legacy_chum5`, which doesn't parse
  spells at all until WP28), confirms the exact 6 SR5-sourced / 4 non-SR5-sourced split, confirms her
  Talent option's `spells` field is 10, and confirms `create_spell_selection` accepts the 6 resolvable
  ids against that budget. The 4 non-resolvable names are asserted disjoint from the catalog
  explicitly, not silently left unchecked.

## Outcome

WP26 complete. `chummer5.data.spells.load_spells()` loads the 93 SR5-core spells as plain roster
data (no `<bonus>`-node parsing needed, unlike qualities/powers — a spell has no chargen-time
computed effect at all); `chummer5.engine.magic.create_spell_selection` validates a Magician's spell
picks against their Talent option's budget, reusing the same "resolve against catalog, reject
duplicates, reject over-budget" shape every other `create_*` function in this engine already has.
`Character.spell_selection` completes the field, staying a single wrapping selection (unlike WP25's
flattened `magic_type`/`magic_rating`/etc.) since nothing reads it piecemeal. Miko's real fixture
data confirms the mechanism's budget (10 spells at letter A) and partially exercises catalog
resolution — 6 of her 10 real spells are SR5-sourced and resolve; the other 4 are Shinto (`SG`)
spells that don't, the same non-core-content gap her own tradition already has, asserted explicitly
rather than glossed over. `pytest`/`ruff`/`mypy` are all green (368 tests, 13 more than before this
WP). WP27 (Adept powers) is the next Phase I engine WP; WP29 is where Talent/tradition/spell/power
selection all reach the browser together.
