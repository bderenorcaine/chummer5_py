# WP77 — Augmentation `<bonus>` coverage audit + "Unmodeled real effects" backfill

Status: complete
Ref: [Implementation Plan.md](../Implementation%20Plan.md) — Phase K, WP77.

## Goal

Close the gap WP38's worklog first flagged and WP41's import widening made larger: every
`BonusSupport.NOT_YET_SUPPORTED` cyberware/bioware row is offered for purchase, charged in full,
grants nothing, and appears nowhere in the Project Overview's "Unmodeled real effects" registry.
Documentation and classification only — no engine or catalog change, by the WP's own scope.

The WP entry arrived with an audit already done (by WP41's author, the same day) and an explicit
instruction not to trust it: "a WP that trusts them without re-running the classifier is doing it
wrong."

## Log

### 2026-07-31 — Re-running the audit, which came back exact

Classified all 87 SR5 `load_cyberware_entries()` rows plus all 6 `load_bioware()` rows. **15
`NOT_YET_SUPPORTED`: 14 cyberware + 1 bioware**, splitting 10 (genuinely out of scope) / 5
(computable today) — per row and per count, matching the entry's table with nothing to correct.
Recording that plainly is the point: the instruction anticipated a mismatch being the finding, and
"no mismatch" is equally a finding, just a shorter one.

The one thing the re-run *did* change is confidence in the reachability column, which I derived
rather than took: `{c.name for c in load_cyberware()} | {b.name for b in load_bioware()}` is exactly
what the two browser pickers render, so intersecting it with the 10 gives 5 offered (Bone Lacing ×3,
Smartlink, Damper) and 5 import-only (Dermal Plating, Armor, Internal Air Tank, Skillwires,
Skilljack). All five of the import-only ones are rating-scaled, which is *why* they're import-only —
WP31's flat filter excludes them from the picker, and WP41 widened import to all 87 rows.

### 2026-07-31 — Where the "why unmodeled" sentences were wrong, and how

The entry told me to re-confirm each Bucket B justification against the code rather than copy its
table, and flagged the armor rows as the one to be careful with. It was right about those, and there
was a second one it didn't flag.

**Armor.** "No armor at all" would be wrong: `chummer5.engine.resources.StarterGearItem.armor_rating`
exists (WP21) and holds a bought armor item's own rating. What's missing is a *character-level*
Armor total — nothing sums those ratings, no derived stat reads one, and `ImprovementType` has no
member an `<armor>` bonus could target (checked all 12). That's the sentence three of the ten rows
now carry, and it points a future WP at the right missing piece instead of the wrong one.

**Smartlink, which the entry got wrong in the other direction.** Its table says "+2 dice on ranged
attacks." The book says otherwise, and it took three hops to establish: p. 453 says the implant is
"an implanted version of the smartlink vision enhancement (p. 444)"; p. 444 says the accessory
"works with a smartgun system to give you the full benefit of the system" with no number of its own;
p. 433 (Smartgun System) finally has it — "If you're using a smartlink, the smartgun system
increases the gun's **Accuracy** by 2." Accuracy is a Limit, not a dice pool.

So where did "+2 dice" come from? The legacy engine. Every `ImprovementType.Smartlink` call site in
`legacy/Chummer/Backend/Equipment/Weapon.cs` (from ~line 9156) sits inside
`if (WirelessOn && HasWirelessSmartgun)` and adds to `decDicePoolModifier`. The entry described the
*code* accurately and the *book* inaccurately. Nothing turns on it — this engine has no weapons,
Accuracy, attack tests or wireless state, so both readings are equally absent — but a registry whose
job is to be the one place you can look this up should not repeat a claim that's wrong about SR5.
The row says both, and the learnings entry says why.

**Skillwires/Skilljack turned out to be inert twice over.** "No skillsoft subsystem" understates it:
the softs themselves aren't purchasable either. SR5's `Skillsofts`-category gear (Activesoft
`Rating * 5000`, Knowsoft `Rating * 2000`, Linguasoft `Rating * 1000`) is all Rating-scaled cost, so
WP21's flat-cost filter drops every one of them from the gear catalog. A player can't buy a soft for
these implants to run. Worth checking rather than assuming, because "no subsystem" and "no content
for the subsystem" would be closed by different future WPs.

### 2026-07-31 — Bucket A is a policy violation, and naming it as one was the whole job

Five rows (`Reaction Enhancers`, `Wired Reflexes`, `Muscle Replacement`, `Voice Modulator`, and the
bioware `Reflex Recorder`) have a `<bonus>` that maps onto an `ImprovementType` this engine already
has. Under the Project Overview's 2026-07-24 policy they must be modeled in full or excluded —
"registered as an accepted gap" is not one of the options, and doing it would launder a violation
into a documented feature. They stay offered and inert in the meantime, deliberately: `Reflex
Recorder` has been in the shipped bioware picker since WP45 and is real content in `Barrett.chum5`'s
import positive case, so excluding it would delete working functionality and break a passing test on
the way to fixing it properly. Recorded as a dated decisions-log entry, which is the only artifact
this WP produces for those five.

The temptation worth naming: Bucket A is the interesting half, and it would have been easy to just
start WP78 here. The reason not to is the reason WP78 is its own WP — it changes a classifier both
cyberware catalogs and the bioware one read, and one of its five rows (`Reflex Recorder`'s
`<selectskill>`) needs a persisted player choice, i.e. save-format and UI work. That does not belong
in a documentation WP.

### 2026-07-31 — A test, not a one-off check, and where the name-lists live

The entry left the choice open and asked for a reason. Test, because the failure mode is entirely in
the future: WP42 widens the picker, or some later WP touches the `<bonus>` grammar, and a row
silently joins one bucket or the other. A one-off check run today catches none of that.

`tests/unit/test_augmentation_unmodeled_registry.py` holds both bucket name-lists as literal
frozensets and asserts the union equals the live classification. Two things about that shape:

- **Literal, not parsed from the markdown table.** Same posture WP56 chose for
  `chummer5.data.qualities._UNMODELED_EFFECT_NOTES` and stated in
  `derived.unmodeled_effect_notes`' docstring: the registry is documentation, mirrored into code by
  hand, never read from disk at runtime.
- **In the test file, not a catalog module**, only because this WP may not touch `src/`. That is the
  one place the no-code-change constraint visibly costs something, and it's what WP79 fixes.

Five tests: the union pin, bucket disjointness (which is WP78's own acceptance criterion, standing
in advance), the 14/1 and 10/5 counts, the 5/5 reachability split, and a check that no registered row
carries a *partially* classified bonus.

### 2026-07-31 — The gap this WP couldn't close, and why it became WP79 rather than a footnote

Writing ten registry rows and then looking at the character sheet is a slightly deflating experience:
none of them appear. WP56 built exactly this mechanism — `Quality.unmodeled_effect_note`, collected
by `derived.unmodeled_effect_notes`, rendered as a collapsible sheet section — and wrote the
collector kind-agnostically (`getattr(item, "unmodeled_effect_note", None)`) precisely so a later
catalog could join in by touching one module. It already walks `cyberware_selection.walk()` and
`bioware_selection.items`. No augmentation catalog populates the field, so a player who buys Bone
Lacing (Titanium) sees an empty section where a Low-Light Vision quality would show a note.

That is one dict per module away, but it is a `src/` change and therefore not this WP's. It isn't
WP78's either (that's the 5 computable rows), and it isn't WP42's (UI only, and it would need the
data-layer half anyway). Left as worklog prose it would be lost, so it's **WP79**, sequenced before
WP42, with the six pre-existing WP38/WP40 augmentation registry rows folded into its scope — theirs
are *partly* modeled, so their note is the remainder, not "this does nothing."

  per-catalog check this WP ran over its own 10, and a wrong label is worse than none — but the
  column is now asymmetric, which will look like an oversight to whoever reads it next unless they
  find this line.
- **The registry is still hand-maintained against `src/`.** This WP added a test that pins
  *which rows must be documented*, not one that pins *that the markdown says so*. The markdown table
  and `REGISTERED_UNMODELED` can still drift apart. Closing that would mean either parsing the table
  at test time (rejected, per WP56) or moving the notes into the catalogs and asserting against them
  — which is WP79, and is the better version of this.
