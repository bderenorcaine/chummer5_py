# WP78 — Model the computable augmentation bonuses

Status: complete
Ref: [Implementation Plan.md](../Implementation%20Plan.md) — Phase K, WP78.

## Goal

Fix WP77's Bucket A: the augmentation rows whose real SR5 effect this engine *can* compute were
being offered, charged in full, and granting nothing — a live departure from the Project Overview's
2026-07-24 "model it in full or exclude it" policy rather than an accepted gap, and one that WP77
was explicitly not allowed to launder into the "Unmodeled real effects" registry.

## Log

### 2026-07-31 — Deciding the WP's size before writing any code

The entry told me to settle `Reflex Recorder`'s membership "early ... rather than letting it quietly
determine the WP's size." That turned out to be the right instruction: the five Bucket A rows are
not five instances of one problem.

Four of them (`Reaction Enhancers`, `Wired Reflexes`, `Muscle Replacement`, `Voice Modulator`) carry
a **fixed** bonus — a named attribute, a named skill, Initiative Dice — whose whole implementation is
"widen a classifier, map three strings to `ImprovementType`s." Nothing about a purchase needs to be
remembered beyond its rating, which the tree engine already carries.

`Reflex Recorder` carries `<selectskill limittoattribute="BOD,AGI,REA,STR"><val>1</val>
</selectskill>` — a **choice made at purchase time**. That needs a chosen skill persisted in
`CharacterSave`, re-validated on load, read out of a legacy `<extra>` element on import
(`Barrett.chum5` really does store `<extra>Longarms</extra>`), and picked in the browser. That is
WP55's four-layer shape, and strictly larger than the other four put together. **WP80**, recorded in
`spec/decisions-log.md` and written out as a full plan entry rather than left as a sentence here.

The thing I made myself check before deferring it: does deferring leave the policy violation open?
Yes — and that's fine, for exactly the reason WP77 already recorded and I re-confirmed rather than
re-argued: `Reflex Recorder` has shipped in the bioware picker since WP45 and is real content in
`Barrett.chum5`'s import positive case, so *excluding* it would delete working functionality and
break a passing test on the way to fixing it properly.

### 2026-07-31 — Enumerating the `<bonus>` shapes before widening the whitelist

Ran a one-off `ElementTree` pass over all 87 SR5 `cyberware.xml` rows, printing every non-empty
`<bonus>`'s child tags **plus the attributes on the `<bonus>` element and on each child**. That last
part is what made this WP correct rather than merely passing, and I would have missed it by only
looking at tags:

```
Reaction Enhancers   bonusattrs={}                  kids=['specificattribute']                          childattrs=[{'precedence': '0'}]
Wired Reflexes       bonusattrs={}                  kids=['disablequality','initiativepass','specificattribute']  childattrs=[{}, {'precedence':'0'}, {'precedence':'0'}]
Muscle Replacement   bonusattrs={'unique':'muscle'} kids=['disablequality','specificattribute','specificattribute'] childattrs=[{}, {}, {}]
Voice Modulator      bonusattrs={}                  kids=['specificskill']                              childattrs=[{}]
```

The same pass also confirmed the widened whitelist reclassifies **exactly** these four and nothing
else: no other SR5 row carries a `<specificattribute>`, `<specificskill>` or `<initiativepass>`.

### 2026-07-31 — `precedence`/`unique` are the load-bearing half, and the fixtures say so

Chasing `precedence` into `legacy/Chummer/Backend/Improvements/AddImprovementAsyncCollection.cs`:
every handler starts from a collection-wide `_strUnique` (read from `<bonus unique="...">`) and
overwrites it with `"precedence" + value` when the child carries a `precedence` attribute. That is
legacy's `Improvement.UniqueName` — the same field this project's `Improvement.unique_name` already
implements as "highest bonus wins."

Then the rulebook, p. 455, which states the rule outright twice on one spread:

> Reaction enhancers are incompatible with all other enhancements to Reaction, including wired
> reflexes.

> Wired reflexes are incompatible with augmentations that affect Reaction or Initiative.

And then the oracle. `SCSi.chum5` owns **both** at Rating 1, over a natural REA of 2 (`<base>1` on
a Human minimum of 1), and its legacy-computed REA `<totalvalue>` is **3**. Without carrying
`precedence` through, this engine would have produced 4 — wrong per the book *and* a diff against
the corpus. That single number is why the dedup marker is in the data layer at all, and it is the
sharpest fixture check this WP has.

`Muscle Replacement`'s `unique="muscle"` is the same mechanism from the other direction: p. 455's
"cannot be combined with other augmentations to the muscles, including muscle augmentation or muscle
toner bioware" — and both of those bioware rows carry the same `unique="muscle"` in `bioware.xml`.
They're rating-scaled and so outside the offered bioware catalog today, but modeling the marker
costs nothing and means the rule is already right when they arrive.

One trap avoided: AGI and STR share the `"muscle"` group but are **different targets**, and
`ImprovementIndex.resolve` dedups per `(improvement_type, target)`. So both apply in full, which is
what p. 455 says ("increases *both* your Strength and Agility"). A dedup keyed on `unique_name`
alone would have silently dropped one of them.

### 2026-07-31 — Two consequences the printed text settles that the XML does not

- **`<initiativepass>` is Initiative *Dice*.** The tag name is an SR4 holdover; p. 455 says "+1D6
  Initiative Die," and legacy's `initiativepass` handler is a one-line delegation to
  `initiativedice`. So it maps to `ImprovementType.INITIATIVE_DICE`, not `INITIATIVE`.
- **Reaction Enhancers' "(and remember to adjust Initiative and Physical limit accordingly)" is a
  consequence, not a second bonus.** Both fall out of `derived.initiative_value`/`physical_limit`
  reading `attribute_value("REA")`. Adding Improvements for them would double-count. The unit test
  asserts they move anyway, because "it follows automatically" is a claim worth checking once.

### 2026-07-31 — `<disablequality>` stayed out of scope, and the guid resolved

The entry flagged that `Wired Reflexes` and `Muscle Replacement` both carry a `<disablequality>`,
that one names `Celerity` and the other "a raw guid — resolve it and confirm before relying on
this," and that a real SR5 quality behind it must be *registered*, not half-modeled.

`3564b678-7721-4a8d-ac79-1600cf92dc14` is **Lightning Reflexes**, `source == "RF"`. So, exactly like
`Celerity`, it is absent from the SR5 quality catalog on source grounds and unreachable by any
character this engine can build — nothing to register, and `_VERIFIED_INERT_BONUS_TAGS`' existing
strip drops nothing real. WP31's docstring had only verified the by-name case, so the by-guid one is
now written down too, with a test pinning the resolution so it can't rot into an unchecked claim.

### 2026-07-31 — Two engine gaps found, and the decision not to fix either here

Both are recorded as dated `spec/decisions-log.md` entries and as full plan entries (WP81 for the
first), because leaving them as worklog prose would lose them.

**The augmented attribute maximum.** `Character.attribute_value` caps
`base + points + Σ improvements` at `attribute_range.maximum` — the *natural* maximum. SR5 allows an
augmented attribute to exceed it by up to +4 (legacy `CharacterAttrib.AttributeModifiers` clamps Σ
attribute improvements to `MetatypeAugmentedMaximum - MetatypeMaximum`; `CalculatedTotalValue` then
clamps to `TotalAugmentedMaximum`). `AttributeRange.augmented` is loaded and read nowhere.

I nearly fixed this — it looks like a one-line change. It isn't:
`create_karma_attribute_selection` enforces SR5's "only one Mental or Physical attribute may sit at
its natural maximum" rule via `attribute_value(code) >= attribute_range(code).maximum`, which
becomes silently wrong the moment `attribute_value` can exceed that maximum. A correct fix is a
natural-vs-augmented split across `Character`, `derived` and the sheet. **WP81.**

Worth being precise about what this WP therefore ships: the gap is **pre-existing**, not introduced
here — WP27's `Improved Physical Attribute` adept power has produced `ImprovementType.ATTRIBUTE`
improvements since Phase I and hits the same ceiling. WP78 makes it reachable from a second
direction, which is why it gets written down now. And none of the three fixtures checked reaches the
boundary (BLUE 3/6, SCSi 3/6, Fuzzy 6/6 — the last sits exactly *on* it), so the oracle has nothing
to say either way.

**`precedence0` suppression vs. addition.** Legacy's `ValueOf` (~line 1096) computes a precedence0
group as `max(Σ unmarked, highest precedence0 + Σ precedence-1)`; this engine computes
`Σ unmarked + highest per unique_name`. They agree whenever every competing bonus carries the marker
— which is the case the rulebook actually names, and the case `SCSi.chum5` proves. They diverge only
when an *unmarked* bonus to the same target coexists: an adept with `Improved Physical Attribute`
(REA) who also buys one of these implants. Closing it means teaching `resolve()` a magic
`"precedence0"` string, i.e. pushing a legacy serialization detail into the engine primitive WP5 and
WP39 deliberately kept clean. Recorded rather than closed, with the modeled half being the one the
book states outright.

### 2026-07-31 — What the shape of `CyberwareFlatBonus` became

The entry left "new field or sibling type" open. **Widened**, not siblinged, and the deciding reason
is that the two new fields are both *defaultable*: `target_name: str = ""` and
`unique_name: str | None = None`. Every existing construction site and every existing test compiles
unchanged. A sibling type would have forced `cyberware_bonus_improvements` to branch on which shape
it got, for no gain.

The neat part fell out of the data layer's existing convention rather than being designed: the
character-wide kinds (`physical_cm`, `initiative_dice`) leave `target_name` at `""`, which *is* the
`_GLOBAL_TARGET` sentinel — so `target=flat_bonus.target_name` covers both the global and the
per-attribute/per-skill case in one expression, with no branch. `chummer5.data.bioware.
BiowareFlatBonus` and `chummer5.data.qualities.FlatBonus` already made the same choice; this is the
third module to land on it.

`value` became `int | None`, `None` meaning "the purchased Rating," mirroring
`CyberwareConditionalBonus.value` exactly — including its `resolve_value(rating)` reader, so the two
bonus types are read the same way. All four rows this WP models are `Rating`-valued.

**A skill *name* is the target, not an id** — the entry asked how a name resolves to a skill id, and
the answer is that it doesn't need to: `ImprovementType.SKILL` already resolves against `skill.name`
everywhere in this engine (`chummer5.engine.skills.skill_rating`, and every producer since WP8).
`Impersonation` confirmed present in `skills.xml`.

## Follow-up

- **WP80** (new): `Reflex Recorder`'s purchase-time skill choice — the last offered-and-inert
  augmentation row, and the one that empties `MODELED_BUT_UNCLASSIFIED` for the first time.
- **WP81** (new): the natural-vs-augmented attribute maximum. Not blocking WP42, but every attribute
  figure this project shows is subtly capped wrong until it lands.
- **A question WP80 must answer that is bigger than WP80.** Legacy treats a `<specificskill>` bonus
  *without* `<applytorating>` as a **dice pool** modifier, not a rating one; this engine routes
  every skill Improvement through `skill_rating`, where it is capped at `MAX_SKILL_RATING_CREATE`
  (6). So Voice Modulator Rating 6 on Impersonation 6 gives 6 here and 12 in legacy. This is
  pre-existing and engine-wide — Enhanced Articulation (WP45), every focus skill bonus (WP47), every
  Mentor Spirit slot (WP48) and Voice Control (WP27) share it — so it was out of scope to change
  under one content WP, but WP78 is what made it *reachable at a magnitude where it bites*. WP80's
  entry names it as a decide-and-document deliverable; if the answer is "these need a real pool
  modifier," that is its own WP, not WP80's.
- **`Muscle Replacement`'s `unique="muscle"` has no partner in scope yet.** Muscle Augmentation and
  Muscle Toner are rating-scaled bioware, excluded from `chummer5.data.bioware`'s 6-row catalog. The
  dedup group is therefore correct but untested against a real collision; whichever WP widens the
  bioware catalog gets the first real check of it.
