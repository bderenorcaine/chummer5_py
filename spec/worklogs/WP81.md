# WP81 — Natural vs. augmented attribute maximum

Status: complete
Ref: [Implementation Plan.md](../Implementation%20Plan.md) — Phase K, WP81.

## Goal

Let an Improvement raise an attribute above its metatype maximum, up to SR5's augmented maximum,
without breaking the chargen rules that read `attribute_value` as the *natural* rating. Claimed by
WP78 on 2026-07-31 as "Gap 1": `Character.attribute_value` clamped `base + points + Σ improvements`
at `attribute_range.maximum`, so a character at REA 6 buying Reaction Enhancers 3 got 6 here and 9
in SR5, and `AttributeRange.augmented` had been loaded since WP2 and read nowhere.

## Log

### 2026-08-01 — The rulebook decided the shape before any code was written

Two passages, both in Step Six, both already condensed in
`spec/rulebooks/character_creation_chapter.md` and pinned verbatim here via `pdftotext -layout -f 96
-l 97` (book p. N = PDF p. N+2). Full transcription in `spec/rulebooks/learnings/phase-k.md`'s WP81
entry; what they settled:

**p. 94 — the cap is on the bonus, not on the total's distance past the natural maximum.** "Each
attribute rating (Mental and Physical) can only receive an augmentation bonus of up to +4. If the
attribute being raised has not reached its natural maximum limit, the attribute can be raised
naturally with Karma; but at no point can augmentations exceed the +4 bonus cap." That second
sentence is the load-bearing one: it says explicitly that reaching the natural maximum and receiving
the augmentation are *independent*, which is precisely what the pre-WP81 single clamp conflated.

**"Mental and Physical" is the whole scope, and the data already encodes it.** Edge is neither, and
every SR5-core metatype's `<edgaug>` equals its `<edgmax>` (Human 7/7, the other four 6/6);
`<magaug>`/`<resaug>` likewise (6/6). So the headroom for EDG/MAG/RES is zero and an improvement
targeting one is clamped to nothing — no special case needed anywhere. This is also why MAG ends up
with no natural/augmented split at all in this engine: it *cannot* be pushed past its natural
maximum, so `natural_attribute_value("MAG")` deliberately returns `attribute_value("MAG")` rather
than inventing a second MAG formula alongside WP35's Essence-loss one.

**The `+4` never needed hardcoding.** `AttributeRange.augmented` is exactly `maximum + 4` on all
eight primary attributes of all five SR5-core metatypes. `attribute_augmented_maximum` returns the
data field; the rule falls out. A hardcoded constant would have been a second source of truth for
something `metatypes.xml` already says, and would have needed its own EDG/MAG carve-out.

**p. 95 — the caller audit, printed.** "Attributes boosted by cyberware or bioware do not affect the
calculation for things such as points for Knowledge skills or Contacts. However, other in-game
mechanics such as Initiative and Inherent Limits are modified by these augmentations." The WP entry
asked for "every existing caller audited for which one it actually wants"; this sentence answers it
for the only three that aren't obviously augmented, and legacy corroborates from its own direction
(`ContactPointsExpression` defaults to `{CHAUnaug} * 3`, `KnowledgeSkillPointsExpression` to
`({INTUnaug} + {LOGUnaug}) * 2`).

**p. 95 also supplied the display.** "Record augmentation ratings in parentheses next to the natural
attribute rating (e.g. Strength 4 (6) for a natural 4 plus muscle augmentation 2)." So the sheet and
the attribute form render `4 (6)`, collapsing to a bare `4` when the two are equal.

### 2026-08-01 — Why a second method, and not a flag or a tuple

The entry left the shape open (a second method, a named tuple, or an `augmented=` flag). The
deciding question turned out not to be aesthetics but **which reading gets to be the default**.

`attribute_value` has roughly 40 call sites across `src/` and `tests/`. Of those, three want the
natural rating: free Knowledge points, free contact points, and the "only one Mental or Physical
attribute at its natural maximum" Karma rule. Every other one — every dice pool, Initiative, all
three Limits, both condition monitors, the Magic-rating chain, the foci/spirit caps — wants the
augmented total.

A flag (`attribute_value(code, augmented=True)`) makes all ~37 of those restate a choice they have
no opinion about, and makes "forgot to pass it" a silently wrong number rather than a line nobody
wrote. A tuple return breaks every existing call site at once for the benefit of three. A second
method leaves the common case untouched, gives the rare case a name that says what it is at the call
site, and makes the diff of this WP legible: the three lines that changed are exactly the three
rules that needed to change. `attribute_augmented_maximum(code)` is a third, one-line method purely
so the ceiling has a name where it is used, instead of `attribute_range(code).augmented` appearing
in engine code that has no other business reaching into the data layer.

### 2026-08-01 — The karma rule was the reason this wasn't a one-line change, and it was subtle

`create_karma_attribute_points`'s "only one at its natural maximum" check read
`candidate.attribute_value(code) >= candidate.attribute_range(code).maximum`. Pre-WP81 that was
*accidentally* correct: since `attribute_value` was itself clamped at the natural maximum, an
augmented attribute and a naturally-maxed one were literally the same number, so `>=` couldn't tell
them apart and didn't need to. The moment the clamp moves, the same expression starts counting an
augmented REA 8 (natural 5) as an attribute sitting at its natural maximum — which would reject
perfectly legal Karma spends, on a route the web layer calls on *every* attribute submission.

The fix is one word (`natural_attribute_value`), but it is the whole reason the WP entry insisted
this couldn't be a cap change in isolation, and it is pinned by a test that asserts both halves: the
augmented attribute does not count, and the rule still fires when a genuine second natural maximum
would be reached.

### 2026-08-01 — The corpus had something to say, and it disagreed with the obvious test

The WP entry asked for `tests/integration/test_character_fixtures.py`'s existing skip of augmented
attributes to be revisited. That test builds each fixture with an empty `ImprovementIndex`, so it
still cannot assert the *number* for a row this engine doesn't model the source of. But it can now
assert the two invariants WP81 implements, against the only real legacy-computed augmented
attributes this project has — so `test_cross_corpus_augmented_totals_respect_the_augmented_maximum`
does exactly that.

Writing it produced the one genuine surprise of this WP. 22 rows across the mundane corpus are
augmented. All 22 respect the augmented maximum. Only **17 respect the `+4` headroom** — and the 5
that don't are all AGI or STR, all on the three fixtures that own cyberlimbs (Bastion's natural AGI
of 1 against a legacy total of 8; Blindfire's natural AGI 2 against 9).

Reading `CalculatedTotalValueCore` explains it and vindicates the implementation rather than
contradicting it: legacy clamps Σ improvements to `+4` inside `AttributeModifiers`, and then, for
the two `Cyberware.CyberlimbAttributeAbbrevs` (AGI, STR) only, **replaces** the resulting value
outright with a per-limb weighted average before applying the single remaining
`TotalAugmentedMaximum` clamp. A cyberlimb average is bounded by the augmented maximum and by
nothing else. This engine has no per-limb attribute system at all — already registered in the
Project Overview's "Unmodeled real effects" table by WP38 for Customized/Enhanced Agility/Strength —
so it can never produce such a total. The test excludes AGI/STR from the headroom half of its check
and says why, rather than asserting a rule that isn't universal or quietly loosening the bound to
whatever the corpus happens to contain.

Worth flagging for whoever eventually models cyberlimbs: the average is applied *after* the `+4`
clamp and *replaces* it, so bolting cyberlimbs onto today's `attribute_value` by adding another
`ATTRIBUTE` improvement would be structurally wrong, not just numerically off.

### 2026-08-01 — Two behavioural changes, both intended, both in tests that had to move

`pytest` went from 1173 to 1183, with exactly two pre-existing assertions changing:

1. `test_attribute_value_caps_at_metatype_maximum_even_with_improvements` asserted `== 6` — the
   precise behaviour this WP exists to remove. Renamed to
   `test_improvements_raise_an_at_maximum_attribute_past_its_natural_maximum` and now asserts both
   sides (natural 6, augmented 9), with a docstring saying what it used to claim and why that was
   wrong.
2. `test_power_selection_select_attribute_shape_raises_the_chosen_attribute` asserted the sheet
   renders `<td>3</td>` for an adept's Improved-Physical-Attribute BOD. The *total* is unchanged
   (natural 1 + 2, both before and after); only the notation moved, to `1 (3)`. The same test now
   also checks the attribute-allocation form uses the same notation, and that an unaugmented row
   still renders a bare number — the common case must stay quiet.

Nothing else in 1173 tests moved, which is the useful signal: the augmented clamp is unreachable for
every character the corpus and the synthetic fixtures actually build, because none of them stacks an
attribute improvement onto an attribute already at its metatype maximum. That is also why WP78 could
ship four attribute-granting implants without tripping over this.

## Follow-up

- **The natural/augmented split now exists but is only drawn for attributes.** WP82 asks the same
  question of skills and answers it differently — see its own worklog for why the display half does
  *not* mirror this one.
- **Cyberlimbs, if they are ever modeled, do not fit the `ATTRIBUTE` improvement shape** (see the
  corpus section above). Whoever picks that up should start from
  `CalculatedTotalValueCore`'s ordering, not from this WP's clamp.
- **`AttributeRange.augmented` is now read, so metavariant data matters more than it did.** Every
  metavariant is out of MVP scope, but if one is ever offered, its own `<xxxaug>` values come along
  for free — nothing in this WP assumes `maximum + 4`.
- **Still nobody's WP: WP78's "Gap 2" (`precedence0` suppression vs. plain addition).** Untouched;
  it lives one layer below the clamp, in `ImprovementIndex.resolve`.
