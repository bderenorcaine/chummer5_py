# WP82 — Natural vs. augmented skill rating

Status: complete
Ref: [Implementation Plan.md](../Implementation%20Plan.md) — Phase K, WP82.

## Goal

WP81's question, asked of skills. `chummer5.engine.skills.skill_rating` returned
`min(purchased points + Σ improvements, MAX_SKILL_RATING_CREATE)`, so a skill augmentation's bonus
was silently swallowed once the purchased rating reached 6 — a `Reflex Recorder` bought for a
rating-6 skill cost 14,000¥ and 0.1 Essence and changed nothing. Claimed by WP80 on 2026-07-31,
which made it reachable at a magnitude where it bites.

## Log

### 2026-08-01 — The entry's one real open question, answered by two pages that disagree about 6

The WP entry was explicit that the deliverable was not "change 6 to something" but "establish what
actually bounds an augmented skill rating." `pdftotext -layout -f 131 -l 133` (book pp. 129-131,
verbatim in `spec/rulebooks/learnings/phase-k.md`) produced two passages:

- **p. 131**, the Skill Rating Table: "RATING 6: PROFESSIONAL. … This is the maximum skill level for
  starting player characters" — and then RATING 7: VETERAN, 8: EXPERT, 9, 10, 11, and 12-13: APEX.
- **p. 129**: "The skill Rating is a numerical value ranging from 1 … to 12 (or 13 with the Aptitude
  quality) representing the height of sentient achievement."

So 6 bounds *starting characters*, i.e. purchases, and the scale itself stops at 12. That is exactly
the shape WP80's finding predicted, and it means `MAX_SKILL_RATING_CREATE` doesn't move at all — it
stays on `create_skill_allocation` and `create_karma_skill_selection`, which read purchased points
directly and were never in scope. What changes is `skill_rating`'s own clamp, to a new
`MAX_SKILL_RATING = 12`.

**Legacy was consulted second, as the entry directs, and it declines to bound the total.**
`CharacterSettings` carries both numbers (`_intMaxSkillRatingCreate = 6`, `_intMaxSkillRating = 12`),
and `Skill.RatingMaximum` — 6 while the character is uncreated — bounds `Base` and `Karma`
individually. But `Skill.TotalBaseRating` is `LearnedRating + RatingModifiers(Attribute)` with no
clamp, and `Skill.Rating` is `Math.Max(CyberwareRating, TotalBaseRating)`, also unclamped. So the 12
this project applies to the augmented total is a **deliberate divergence, not a port**, and the
comment on the constant says so. Justification: p. 129 defines the scale as stopping there, nothing
in SR5 says what a Rating 14 skill would mean, and it costs nothing — the largest stack today's
catalogs can produce is far short of 12, which is why the test that exercises it needs an artificial
+20 improvement. It is a guard-rail against a future catalog widening.

**Aptitude's 13 is deliberately not modeled.** Legacy implements Aptitude as a per-skill
`Improvement.Maximum` term folded into `RatingMaximum` — i.e. it raises the *purchase* cap, not the
scale ceiling. This project has no `Improvement.Maximum` field at all, and `Aptitude` classifies
`NOT_YET_SUPPORTED` in the quality catalog, so no character this engine builds can hold it. Two
independent reasons not to build the machinery speculatively.

### 2026-08-01 — The clamp was worse than the entry said: it was deleting dice, not just implants

WP80's framing was "a Reflex Recorder bought for a rating-6 skill does nothing." True, but it
understated the blast radius, and lifting the clamp is what showed it. Two synthetic fixtures failed
immediately:

- **Renata** (WP72's reference mundane): Athletics group 5 + Natural Athlete's +2 → Gymnastics and
  Running each read 6, not 7. Both dice pools were a die short.
- **Sabine** (WP74's combat mage): Talent grant 5 + the Mountain mentor's Magician slot +2 →
  Counterspelling read 6, not 7. Same missing die in the pool.

Neither of those bonuses is an augmentation. Every skill Improvement this project grants — quality
bonuses, Mentor Spirit slots, foci, the Talent free-skill grant, Enhanced Articulation, Voice
Modulator — was being truncated the moment it landed on a skill at or near 6. Both fixtures' own
`expected` values *and* their citation strings said "capped at 6", so the bug was written down as
intended behaviour in two places; correcting them was part of this WP.

This is the strongest argument that the WP was worth doing at all. The Reflex Recorder case that
motivated it is a rare purchase; "any skill bonus on a skill at 5-6" is not.

### 2026-08-01 — Why the skill display says "bonuses" and the attribute display says "augmented"

The entry asks the display to mirror "whatever WP81 settles on for attributes." It mirrors the
*notation* — `bought (total)`, collapsing to a bare number when equal, on both the character sheet
and the skill picker — but deliberately not the *label*, and this took the most thought of anything
in the WP.

WP81 can call the parenthesis "augmented" because the attribute split has two clean sides: points
bought from a budget, and `ImprovementType.ATTRIBUTE` records that are all cyberware, bioware or
adept powers. `ImprovementType.SKILL` is not that clean. It currently carries three genuinely
different things:

1. The Magician/Adept **Talent free-skill grant** — a granted *natural* rating, which WP25 routed
   through the Improvement index purely because `skill_rating`'s existing formula would then sum it
   for free (that module's own docstring says as much).
2. Quality and Mentor Spirit bonuses whose **printed effect is a dice-pool modifier**, not a rating
   one (Natural Athlete: "+2 dice pool modifier for Running and Gymnastics skill tests", p. 393).
3. Genuine **rating augmentations** — `Reflex Recorder`, p. 460, the one row the book states as a
   Rating bonus outright.

Labelling the delta "augmented" would assert a classification of (1) and (2) that WP80's
decisions-log entry explicitly recorded as still open and still not one WP's job. "Rating (with
bonuses)" is true of all three. The resulting numbers are honest either way — a Talent-granted
Spellcasting reads `0 (4)`, which is a fair statement that no skill points were spent on it, and a
Catlike-boosted Sneaking reads `0 (2)`, which is arguably closer to SR5's own model (rating 0, +2
dice) than the bare `2` it showed before.

I considered dropping the split for skills entirely on those grounds. Against it: ratings can now
exceed 6, so a bare `7` next to a "Points" input reading `5` is *more* confusing than before, not
less — the split is what makes the new behaviour legible. Seven existing web tests moved to the new
format; none of them changed a computed number.

### 2026-08-01 — What did not need to change, and the confirmation that matters

The entry's own prediction held exactly: `create_skill_allocation` and `create_karma_skill_selection`
both read purchased points directly rather than going through `skill_rating`, so both were
natural-only by construction and needed no edit. That is the opposite of WP81's situation, where
`create_karma_attribute_selection` genuinely read the augmented value and would have started
misbehaving.

Pinned rather than assumed: a test asserts that a character whose augmented Archery is already 7 (5
bought + a 2-point Improvement) may still Karma-buy the 6th point and still may not buy the 7th. The
Improvement must not consume headroom a purchase is entitled to, in either direction.

`chummer5.engine.knowledge` was left alone. Its Knowledge/Language ratings share the same
`MAX_SKILL_RATING_CREATE` purchase cap, but nothing in this project produces a Knowledge-skill
Improvement, so there is no augmented side to split off yet.

## Follow-up

- **Rating vs. pool is now the load-bearing open question, not a curiosity.** Before WP82 the clamp
  hid it; now every skill bonus visibly contributes to a rating that can read 7+. Whoever takes it
  needs a per-row reading of every skill bonus this engine grants (quality, Mentor Spirit, focus,
  Talent grant, Enhanced Articulation, Voice Modulator, Voice Control) — the printed wording differs
  row to row, and the XML shape does not distinguish them. WP80's decisions-log entry is the
  starting point; this WP's display label is the second thing that would change.
- **The Talent free-skill grant is the odd one out and should probably stop being an Improvement.**
  It is a granted natural rating modelled as a bonus, which is why a Magician's sheet reads
  `0 (4)`. Moving it onto `SkillAllocation` (or a sibling field) would make both the display and the
  rating-vs-pool audit simpler; deliberately not attempted here, since it touches Phase I's own
  save/load and web shape.
- **`MAX_SKILL_RATING` is currently unreachable.** If a future WP widens a catalog such that it
  starts biting, that is the signal to check whether legacy's "no clamp at all" is the better
  reading after all — the divergence is recorded on the constant itself, not buried here.
- **Knowledge/Language skills have the same split waiting.** Nothing produces a Knowledge Improvement
  today; the first WP that adds one inherits this exact question.
