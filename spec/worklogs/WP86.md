# WP86 — Harness: express a cyberware purchase tree

Phase S. Closes the *harness half* of coverage gap **G8** — cyberware bought as a tree, with ratings,
plugins and Capacity budgets. Tests-only; the engine has had all of this since WP38/WP41/WP42.

## Goal

`SyntheticCharacter.cyberware` was a flat `tuple[str, ...]` that `build_synthetic()` turned into
rating-1, plugin-free `CyberwarePurchaseRequest`s. Widen it so a fixture can say what the engine
actually builds and what a player actually buys.

## Log

### The finding worth recording: an input-format limit reads as full coverage from inside the table

`synthetic.py`'s own docstring said it plainly and nobody read it as a gap: *"Each name is bought as
a zero-plugin purchase tree at rating 1 (WP41); no synthetic fixture installs a plugin yet."* Phase
S's §2 inventory was built by asking which *subsystems* had no fixture. Cyberware had one (WP73's
samurai), so the row was checked off. What §2 had no way to ask was whether the harness could
*express* what the subsystem does — and it couldn't, so every fixture bought flat rating-1 leaves and
every fixture therefore reported `initiative_dice: 1` and an unaugmented REA, consistently and
wrongly-in-aggregate. The generalisable lesson (already logged in `decisions-log.md` from the
planning pass, restated here from the implementing side): the cheapest way to find a limitation like
this is to feed the system an artifact authored by somebody who wasn't looking at your coverage
table.

### Shape: `CyberwarePick`, with bare names still legal

```python
CyberwarePick(name: str, rating: int = 1, plugins: tuple[CyberwarePick | str, ...] = ())
```

and `cyberware: tuple[CyberwarePick | str, ...]`. Three choices worth stating:

- **A bare `"Datajack"` still means exactly what it always did.** WP73-76's literals are untouched,
  which was an explicit acceptance criterion and also just correct: a rating-1, plugin-free purchase
  is the common case, and forcing every one of them into a constructor call would make the *typical*
  entry noisier to buy the *atypical* one.
- **A plugin may itself be a bare name.** Cyberears' four plugins include two rating-1 items; writing
  them as strings keeps the nested literal readable at three levels deep.
- **Declaring a bundled subsystem is an error, not a duplicate.** Cyberears bundles Sound Link free
  and Cybereyes Basic System bundles Image Link; `create_cyberware_tree_selection` installs those
  itself. A fixture naming one would buy a *second* copy at full price, silently inflating both the
  nuyen total and the Capacity spend — the exact two figures the fixture exists to pin down. The WP
  entry called for this specifically; `cyberware_purchase_request()` raises with the item name and
  the reason.

### Metric keys: a path, and why an ambiguous one raises

`cyberware:<name path>:capacity_{budget,spent,remaining,cost}`, where the path is `/`-separated
catalog names — `Cyberears`, `Cyberears/Audio Enhancement`.

The point of `capacity_remaining` deserves stating: `create_cyberware_tree_selection` already rejects
a tree whose plugins *overflow* a parent. What it has never had any way to say is that they fill it
**exactly**, which is what both of razorboy's Rating-3 parents do (12/12, `Rating * 4`). "Not
rejected" and "exactly full" are very different claims about a boundary, and only the second one
notices if a Capacity formula quietly gains or loses a point.

An ambiguous path — two purchases of the same name, which is legal (a matched pair of cyberlimbs) —
raises rather than answering for the first match. A fixture that means one of two identical limbs
needs a way to say *which*, and inventing an index before any fixture needs one would be building
past the ask. The same "extend when a fixture needs it" discipline every field on
`SyntheticCharacter` already follows.

### Proof: structural equality against the real save

The acceptance criterion was "the widened field can express `razorboy.json`'s `cyberware_purchases`
exactly." Asserted as `[cyberware_purchase_request(p) for p in RAZORBOY_CYBERWARE] ==
list(save.cyberware_purchases)` — structural equality on the request tree, not merely matching
totals. Two different trees can coincidentally cost the same; it's the *shape* (which plugin in which
parent, at what rating) that WP73-76 couldn't say. A second test additionally runs the same picks
through `build_synthetic()` and reproduces Essence 5.90, ¥260,000 and both 12/12 budgets.

That second test reuses `DECKER_FIXTURE` with a couple of fields replaced (Resources A so the tree
fits without WP87's gear/Lifestyle fields, Attributes E to keep the Sum-to-Ten total at exactly 10).
Slightly inelegant, and deliberate: WP86 is not allowed to author a whole character — that's WP88 —
so it borrows one.
