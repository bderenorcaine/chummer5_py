# WP95 — Unmodeled-effect registry: the rigger surface

Phase P's fifth WP, and the one that makes the phase honest. WP91-94 gave a player vehicles, drones
and a Matrix stat block. This one says, on the character sheet, what none of that actually does.

## Goal

Every rigger-reachable aspect this project offers gets a row in Project Overview's "Unmodeled real
effects" table *and* an `unmodeled_effect_note` a player sees: `Control Rig`, the four gear
categories (RCCs, cyberdecks, commlinks, autosofts), and every vehicle/drone.

## Log

### The mechanism was less "already exists" than the WP's own notes assumed

The WP entry says the collector "already walks `resources_selection.gear` via `getattr`, so only the
catalog side is missing." True for gear. Not true for vehicles: `chummer5.engine.derived
.unmodeled_effect_notes` walks a **hand-written list of `Character` fields**, and
`vehicle_selection` — added by WP92, after the collector was written — was simply not in it. A
perfectly-populated `Vehicle.unmodeled_effect_note` would have rendered nowhere.

That's a generic trap, not a one-off, so it's recorded in three places (the function's docstring,
Project Overview's registry preamble, `spec/decisions-log.md`): **the `getattr` indirection makes a
new *catalog* free; it does not make a new *selection* free.** The reason the omission looked like
coverage is that `Power`/`Focus`/`LifestyleTier`/`Spell` are all walked already and all populate
nothing — the loop list looked exhaustive because most of its entries were no-ops.

### `Control Rig` was invisible to WP77's audit by construction

WP90 §4 found it and called it "100% unmodeled with no registry row." Working out *why* nobody had
noticed is the more useful half. WP77's audit swept the `NOT_YET_SUPPORTED` bucket — rows whose
`<bonus>` node `classify_bonus` declined to parse. `Control Rig` has **no `<bonus>` node at all**,
so it classifies `NO_EFFECT`, sitting with the many rows that genuinely have nothing to model.

So `bonus_support` classifies *parseability*, not *modelledness*, and an audit that treats them as
the same thing has a blind spot the size of the `NO_EFFECT` bucket. Recorded in
`chummer5.data.cyberware`'s docstring, in the registry test, and as a dated ruling. It also means
the row needed a **third** bucket in `tests/unit/test_augmentation_unmodeled_registry.py`
(`FULLY_UNMODELED_WITHOUT_A_BONUS_NODE`) rather than being forced into one of the two existing ones:
that file's central invariant is `NOT_YET_SUPPORTED == REGISTERED | MODELED_BUT_UNCLASSIFIED`, which
is correct and had to keep holding.

Magnitude, for the record: rating 1 is 43,000¥ and `Rating * 1` Essence — a **full sixth of a
character's Essence** — for an implant whose entire printed effect (p. 266) raises a *vehicle's*
limits while jumped in.

### Gear notes are keyed by category

Every other catalog's dict is name-keyed, because each quality's or implant's gap is its own. Gear
isn't like that: all 8 RCCs have one gap (no Matrix), all 6 autosofts have another (no drone tests).
Name-keying would have meant ~30 identical strings with 30 independent chances to drift from the
registry table. Four category keys instead, and the invariant a test can pin gets stronger:
**all-or-none per category**, checked over both loaders' full output.

Vehicles take the same idea further — two module-level strings, chosen on `is_drone` — because the
only per-row difference across all 42 rows is exactly what that flag already records.

### Both gear loaders had to change, and only one was obvious

The four annotated categories split across `load_gear` and `load_rating_gear`: commlinks, cyberdecks
and RCCs are flat-cost; all 6 autosofts are `Rating * 500`, so they are the *rating* loader's rows
and `load_gear` contributes nothing to that category. Pinned as its own test, since "I added the
note to the gear loader" is a very easy way to ship three of four categories.

### The drone/vehicle split is the phase's one real cost, made visible

WP90 §1's finding — drones ship with a rigger interface built in, vehicles don't, and the interface
is a *modification* excluded by the 2026-07-21 ruling — means a player can buy a vehicle no rigger
can use. The ruling stands; this WP is where that stops being invisible. The vehicle note says
"cannot be jumped into as bought" and names the 1,000¥ item and the ruling; the drone note says the
opposite and then names what's still missing anyway.

## Design decisions

- **Three note shapes, one per catalog's actual structure**: name-keyed (cyberware, inherited),
  category-keyed (gear, new), and `is_drone`-selected (vehicles, new). Uniformity here would have
  been uniformity of the mirror-drift risk, not of anything useful.
- **A third bucket in the registry test, not a stretched existing one.** See above.
- **The autosoft note names three gaps, not one.** "No Matrix rules" would have been true and
  useless; p. 269 gives a slot formula, a `[Model]` binding rule and a skill analogy, and none of
  the three is represented.
- **`Control Rig`'s note must not contain the word "modeled."** Pinned by a test: the
  partly-modeled rows' notes all say what *is* applied, and this one has nothing to say it about.

## Traps hit

- **`vehicle_selection` not being walked** — see above; the WP's own "Depends on" notes said the
  opposite, and WP93's worklog had already flagged it. Reading the prior WP's follow-up section is
  what caught it before the acceptance test did.
- **`test_create_resources_selection_within_budget` compared a whole `StarterGearItem` by value.**
  It caught WP94's field addition (good) and then WP95's (a tax). Rewritten to compare against the
  catalog's own row, which is what the test actually means — a flat purchase copies the entry
  verbatim — so it keeps its teeth without needing an edit per field.
- **A note containing an apostrophe renders HTML-escaped** (`drone&#39;s`), so an assertion on the
  rendered page has to either escape too or match a fragment without one. Matched on
  "no autosoft slot accounting" instead.
