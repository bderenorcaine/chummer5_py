# WP96 — The Rigger synthetic fixture

Phase P's last WP, and the one that puts a person in front of everything WP91-95 built. It is also
the first synthetic fixture written from *outside* Phase S: the phase that owns the fixture harness
closed with WP88, having recorded one deliberate hole — "**Known gap: no Rigger** … not a hole to
plug until Phase P lands." Phase P landed. This is the plug.

## Goal

`RIGGER_FIXTURE` in `tests/fixtures/synthetic_fixtures.py`, with the full hand-derived
`expected`/`citations` treatment WP72-76/WP88 established, plus whatever `SyntheticCharacter` fields
and `resolve_metric` keys the vehicle side needs — and a seventh row on Phase S §5's roster table.

## Log

### Designing against §5's table changed the character, twice

The WP's own instruction was to design against the roster ("pick attributes and a metatype that
don't duplicate a peak"). Two decisions came directly out of doing that rather than out of what a
rigger "should" look like:

**Troll.** The set was Human ×3, Ork, Elf, Dwarf — Troll was the one SR5-core metatype missing, and
nothing in the plan had noticed. Taking it is the fixture's largest single coverage win after the
vehicles themselves: Troll is the first metatype in the set whose attribute *minimums* exceed 1
(BOD 5, STR 5) and the first whose Mental maximums are 5 rather than 6. It also constrains the build
hard, which is exactly what a fixture wants — Troll is offered only at Heritage A and B, and a
rigger needs Resources A, so Heritage is forced to B, and Heritage B/Troll grants **0 special
attribute points**: Edge sits on the Troll floor of 1 and nothing in the build can lift it.

**BOD 5 / STR 5 bought with zero points.** The obvious way to write a Troll is to spend on the big
attributes. That would have duplicated Rebar's BOD/STR 6 peak, which the table forbids. Taking the
floors and leaving them there is both legal and better characterisation — she is naturally huge and
is emphatically not the muscle — and it frees all 14 Attributes-D points for REA 6 (a **natural** 6;
Tithe's REA 6 is augmented from a natural 4, so this is still nobody else's peak), LOG 4, INT 4,
AGI 3, WIL 2.

### The Control Rig's rating-2 aside turned out to be the point

The WP entry offers "a full Essence point at rating 1, or two at rating 2" as if the two were
interchangeable. They are not. `<ess>Rating * 1` at rating 2 costs exactly 2.00, so Essence lands on
**exactly 4** — the one shape `ceil(Essence)` has never been asserted in. The set's other five are
6.0 (uncybered), 2.4, 5.7, 5.3 and 0.10, all of which are either "no cyberware" or fractional. And
it is load-bearing rather than decorative: Social Limit reads `ceil(ESS)`, so this is the first
fixture where that `ceil` is the identity function, and a bug that (say) floored instead would pass
every other fixture and fail this one.

Buying anything else chromed would have destroyed the property, which is why she has exactly one
implant. That reads as characterisation (all her money went into the fleet) and is really a test
design constraint.

### `build_synthetic()` never resolved metatype-granted qualities

Found by writing `unmodeled_notes:names` and getting a set that was one row short of what the
character sheet would render: Troll's free Thermographic Vision was missing.

`chummer5.io.save_format.build_character` has called `resolve_metatype_qualities` +
`apply_quality_improvements(..., source_type=ImprovementSource.METATYPE)` since WP23. The synthetic
harness — which follows that function's evaluation order deliberately and closely, and says so in
several docstrings — never did. Six fixtures and four phases of it, unnoticed, because **all four
SR5 metatype-granted qualities are `NO_EFFECT` or `NOT_YET_SUPPORTED`**: Elf/Ork Low-Light Vision,
Dwarf/Troll Thermographic Vision, Dwarf Resistance to Pathogens/Toxins. They apply zero Improvements
and move no number any fixture asserts. WP73's own comment even records the situation without seeing
it as a gap: "Ork metatype grants Low-Light Vision free … not asserted here."

They are inert, not absent: each is a row in the sheet's "Unmodeled real effects" section. Fixed in
the harness; every existing fixture's literals are unchanged, which is precisely why nothing caught
it. Written up in `spec/decisions-log.md` with the general form — a harness only mirrors a
production path as far as someone has written assertions, so a WP adding a metric key should check
what populates it in `build_character` before assuming the harness does.

### A repeated vehicle is not the same problem as a repeated cyberware purchase

`_capacity_metric` raises on an ambiguous path, because two cyberlimbs of the same name can carry
different plugins at different ratings and answering for the first would be silently wrong.
`_vehicle_metric` deliberately does **not**: a `Vehicle` is an immutable catalog row with no
per-purchase state attached at all — no rating, no plugins, no installed anything, which is the
whole content of the stock-only ruling — so two Fly-Spies answer every key identically. Reading the
first is reading both. Same for `_gear_metric`.

Getting this backwards in either direction would have been a real cost: raising would have forced
the fixture to buy exactly one of each row, which would have thrown away the "repeats are legal"
assertion the vehicle catalog most needs.

### Budgeting ¥450,000 is harder than it sounds

Resources A is the first A-letter Resources in the set (nobody else needed it), and 450,000¥ is a
lot of money against a flat-cost gear catalog whose items are mostly in the hundreds. The loadout
was tuned to land inside p. 94's "5,000 nuyen or less" carry-over allowance the way Tithe's ¥580
does; the last ¥34,000 of slack was closed by taking the *best* offerable RCC (Proteus Poseidon,
68,000¥, Availability 12R — exactly on WP94's cap) rather than the mid-range Maersk Spider, and the
remainder by nine months of Medium Lifestyle rather than the usual one. Final: ¥4,255 left.

### What the fixture asserts that nothing else could

- Both Condition Monitor formulas, with the split visible: the Step-Van's `12 + ceil(16/2) = 20`
  against the Kanmushi's `6 + ceil(0/2) = 6`. The Kanmushi's printed **Body 0** is the boundary of
  the formula; the Wasp (Body 10 → 17) and the Fly-Spy (Body 1 → 7) cover the even and odd halves of
  the rounding.
- Ten purchases over seven distinct rows — repeats are legal and mean quantity.
- The Matrix stat block on a *character* (WP90 §3's own ask), not only in a catalog test.
- **Bilingual**, and therefore `native_language_limit == 2` with two natives actually declared.
  `build_synthetic()` has resolved Knowledge *after* qualities since WP87 specifically so that
  Improvement is on the index in time, and until now nothing tested that ordering.
- A third distinct outcome of the skill-group-breaking rule: Gunnery belongs to **no group at all**,
  so raising it with Karma can break nothing. WP75 covers raise-breaks-a-group, WP88 covers
  specialize-breaks-a-group.
- The five-way Step Six ledger (gear + Rating-priced gear + Lifestyle + cyberware + vehicles).
  Tithe reached four of the five.

## Design decisions

- **`vehicles` is name-keyed like every other authoring field**, even though WP92 deliberately chose
  *ids* for the engine API. The split is the harness's standing convention ("fixtures are
  name-keyed, the engine is id-keyed"), and `build_synthetic()` resolves it.
- **Names resolve against the offerable 35-row catalog, not WP91's literal 42.** A fixture naming an
  over-Availability or unprinted row is naming something no player can buy, and should fail in the
  harness with a clear message rather than in the engine as an unknown id.
- **Vehicles resolve third among the shared-pool spenders**, matching `build_character`. The budget
  is read straight off `nuyen_remaining` (already netted of cyberware and bioware by the re-call
  above it) rather than re-subtracting by hand — the same shortcut the foci block already takes.
  `test_rigger_fixtures_vehicle_ledger_agrees_with_the_save_path` rebuilds the same purchase through
  `create_character_save` + `build_character` and compares the bottom line, so the claim is checked
  rather than asserted in a comment.
- **`vehicle:<Name>:<field>` reads printed stats too, not only the derived Condition Monitor.**
  Those stats are catalog constants, but they are what the character sheet renders (WP93), so a
  fixture that pins them is pinning what a player reads.
- **`gear:Proteus Poseidon:programs` is asserted even though WP94 deliberately does not display it**
  (SR5 core prints no program-slot count for an RCC). Cited as such — the loader's value shouldn't
  drift unnoticed just because it stays off the sheet.

## Traps hit

- **`Control Rig` bundles a free Datajack** (`bundled_subsystem_names`). Declaring it would have
  bought a second one at 1,000¥ and 0.1 Essence, which would have destroyed the exactly-4 Essence
  the fixture is built around. `cyberware_purchase_request` rejects it, as WP86 designed.
- **A second attribute at its natural maximum is illegal** (p. 99's "only one"). Troll's LOG maximum
  is 5, so the tempting Step Seven spend of LOG 4 → 5 (25 Karma, almost exactly the pool) would have
  put LOG alongside REA 6 and been rejected by `create_karma_attribute_points`. The Karma went to
  Gunnery and Knowledge instead.
- **Ruff's line-length and import ordering**, twice: `chummer5.engine.vehicles` sorts after
  `chummer5.engine.skills`, and `unmodeled_effect_notes` after `stun_condition_monitor`.
